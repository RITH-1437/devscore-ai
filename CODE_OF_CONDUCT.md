# Code of Conduct

GitRadar is an open-source project built for developers. We want participation to be constructive, respectful, and safe for everyone.

This Code of Conduct applies to all project spaces: the repository, issues, pull requests, discussions, and any other channels where the project is maintained.

## Expected behavior

Contributors and maintainers are expected to:

- Be respectful and professional in communication
- Give constructive feedback focused on the work, not the person
- Welcome newcomers and help them get oriented
- Accept reasonable disagreement and differing technical opinions
- Prioritize the health of the project and its users

## Unacceptable behavior

The following will not be tolerated:

- Harassment, discrimination, or personal attacks
- Trolling, insults, or deliberately inflammatory comments
- Publishing others' private information without permission
- Sexualized language or imagery
- Sustained disruption of issues, reviews, or community discussion
- Retaliation against someone reporting a concern in good faith

## Scope

This policy applies when you represent the project — for example, when using the project issue tracker, submitting code, or interacting as a maintainer on behalf of GitRadar.

It also applies in public spaces when your conduct would reasonably be associated with the project or its community.

## Reporting

If you experience or witness unacceptable behavior, report it privately to the maintainer:

**Email:** neyrithneyrith@gmail.com

Please include:

- What happened and when
- Links to relevant issues, PRs, or comments
- Any other context that helps us understand the situation

Reports are handled confidentially. We will not share your identity without your consent unless required by law.

## Maintainer responsibilities

Maintainers are responsible for:

- Clarifying and enforcing this Code of Conduct
- Reviewing reports promptly and fairly
- Taking appropriate corrective action
- Protecting reporters from retaliation

Maintainers who fail to uphold these standards may be removed from their role by the project owner.

## Enforcement

Maintainers may take any action they deem appropriate, including:

1. **Reminder** — a private note that behavior should change
2. **Warning** — a formal warning with expected corrective action
3. **Temporary restriction** — limited participation for a defined period
4. **Permanent ban** — removal from project spaces for repeated or severe violations

We will document enforcement decisions internally and communicate outcomes to the people involved when appropriate.
